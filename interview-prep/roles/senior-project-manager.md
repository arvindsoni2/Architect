# Senior Project Manager interview guide

**Status:** Draft for owner review · **Last reviewed:** 13 October 2026

Prepare to show control of a defined investment: business case, scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality and transition. Explain governance as a way to enable timely decisions and accountability. Agile delivery can sit within those controls; a project plan should reflect how the work is actually delivered.

## Positioning and evidence

A useful opening: “I have managed technology delivery across regulated banking, utilities and operational software, coordinating stakeholders and distributed teams. I focus on a credible plan, explicit risks and dependencies, controlled change and a supported transition into operations. I use technical understanding to improve estimates and readiness decisions.” Add one actual governance or recovery decision and its result.

## Questions to rehearse

1. **How do you initiate a project?** Confirm the problem, sponsor, business case, measurable benefits, scope boundaries, assumptions, dependencies and governance. Establish a realistic delivery approach and who can accept residual risk or changes.
2. **How do you build a credible plan?** Work with delivery experts on work packages, estimates, dependencies, resource constraints and acceptance. Identify the critical path and uncertainty. Update forecasts from evidence rather than preserving an obsolete baseline.
3. **How do you manage budget and forecast?** Explain approved baseline, committed/actual cost, remaining forecast, contingency rules and forecast at completion. Investigate variance and present options. State which costs and approvals you actually controlled; do not invent a programme budget.
4. **How do you manage scope change?** Describe the request, impact on business value, cost, date, quality and dependencies. Obtain approval at the right level, update the baseline and communicate it. An Agile backlog still needs visibility when changes affect contractual commitments.
5. **What makes RAID useful?** Give each significant item an owner, impact, action and review/decision date. Distinguish a risk from an issue, and an assumption from a known dependency. Explain a decision your RAID process enabled.
6. **How do you manage a fixed regulatory deadline?** Identify the precise obligation with the relevant specialists, minimum compliant scope and evidence needed. Work backwards through testing and approvals; escalate early with options. Regulatory urgency does not justify silently accepting unsafe release risk.
7. **How do you govern suppliers and stakeholders?** Agree deliverables, interfaces, milestones, evidence and escalation rights. Use a short decision paper for sponsor choices. Describe how you managed disagreement without hiding it in a green report.
8. **How do you close and realise benefits?** Confirm acceptance, support ownership, outstanding risks, contracts, documentation and lessons. Assign benefits owners and measurement dates beyond project closure. Savings can be forecast, avoided cost or cash released; say which.

## Worked infrastructure answer

Use the March 2020–May 2022 Northern Powergrid infrastructure initiative to explain one migration or upgrade decision. The portfolio reports 120+ servers and 30+ applications and approximately £400k annual savings. Describe the dependency, outage consequence, sequencing choice, change preparation, rollback and business validation that you personally coordinated. Explain who approved the release and residual risk.

Do not open with “I managed a £400k programme” unless you separately have a verified budget. Annual savings and delivery budget are different. Explain how the savings estimate was produced and whether realised benefits were tracked after transition.

## Governance scenario — illustrative, not historical

A sponsor wants a fixed date, a supplier is two weeks late and the cost forecast is worsening. Validate dependency dates and the remaining critical path. Confirm whether the date is contractual, regulatory or aspirational. Present a short options paper: change scope, sequence differently, add targeted capacity with credible onboarding assumptions, or move the milestone. Include cost, residual risk, affected benefits and approval needed.

Seek a decision before the option expires; do not wait for the next monthly board. Update the plan, forecast and change log after approval. Monitor evidence against the revised milestones. If the sponsor accepts risk, document the actual acceptance and any controls; do not interpret silence as approval.

For an earned-value question, define planned value, earned value and actual cost before using schedule or cost indices. Explain whether the work packages and acceptance evidence support that method. Do not apply a precise index to loosely defined progress percentages or confuse schedule variance with a forecast completion date.

## Credibility probes

What was your approved budget? What changed and who signed it off? What was on the critical path? Which assumption failed? What risk did you escalate? What evidence supported go-live? Who measured benefits after you left? Prepare one imperfect outcome and what you changed subsequently.

Your strongest banking example must be one identifiable initiative. Do not combine GDPR, payments and Open Banking into a single story with unsupported dates or supplier counts.

## Additional scenario drills

These are fictional practice cases. Use the [shared scenario method](../shared/handbook.md#scenario-answers), identifying delegated tolerances, approval and evidence needed to change the baseline.

**SP1 — forecast exceeds approved funding.** New integration work increases the estimate at completion beyond authorised funding; the sponsor still expects the original scope/date. Validate actuals, commitments, remaining estimates, scope and contingency rules with finance and delivery leads. Compare descoping, resequencing and additional funding, showing effects on benefits and risk. Seek the required decision before unfunded commitments are made, then update the approved plan. **Probe:** what if the sponsor refuses to decide? **Weak response:** spending first, hiding the variance or assuming contingency is automatically available.

**SP2 — supplier delay at a fixed compliance deadline.** A key supplier is late and a critical control has not passed verification. Confirm the actual obligation with compliance/legal specialists and identify minimum compliant scope and the remaining dependency chain. Compare an independently verified alternative, scope reduction and a specialist-led contingency for the deadline. Escalate with evidence and record the decision. **Probe:** what if the sponsor says to launch anyway? **Weak response:** treating sponsor pressure as permission to breach a mandatory obligation, or claiming the PM can personally certify compliance.

## 2026 question bank with model answers

Current guides add AI in project management, hybrid methods and distributed teams to the classic questions. PMI's PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition (November 2025) organises the discipline around six principles and seven performance domains — governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources and risk — and adds guidance on AI. [1][2] For UK public-sector panels, map your stories to the Civil Service behaviours. [3] Where a model answer describes a habit rather than a recorded story, keep only what you actually do.

### Q1. Tell me about yourself and your project management background.

**Model answer:** "I've delivered technology projects for twenty years in regulated sectors — utilities and banking through TCS, then food tech at Natoora. I'm strongest where delivery is constrained: a fixed client date, a shared specialist, a production estate that can't go down. At Northern Powergrid I coordinated an infrastructure programme across more than 120 servers and 30 applications, reported to save about £400k a year. At Natoora I took an internal platform to external SaaS customers and ran the rollout of an AI order-automation tool. I hold PMP and PMI-ACP, and I'm looking for a role where governance helps decisions happen faster." Keep it under two minutes. [1]

### Q2. Describe a time a project couldn't meet its plan. How did you handle it?

**Model answer:** Use [B1](../shared/evidence-bank.md#behavioural-stories-owner-supplied-13-october-2026). "Two launches turned out to need the same senior engineer, which neither plan had shown. I gave each sponsor options — delay, reduce scope, phase the rollout — and when neither moved, ran a joint session with the dependency visible and the CTO available to decide. We agreed to finish GenAI first and move multiple basket by two to three weeks, because its go-to-market wasn't final. The lesson: put shared specialists into the dependency map at planning, not when the conflict lands." [1]

### Q3. Walk me through the cost side of a project you ran.

**Model answer:** "On the Natoora SaaS work, implementation investment was about £9k for the first client and £7k for the second. The SaaS work brought a £28k setup fee and £1.6k a month in recurring revenue, and reported £29k profit in its first five months — I don't present those as one customer's figures. I planned team capacity at 70% availability because the same people supported the core platform, and agreed that assumption with the CTO up front. I'd be careful to say these are two different customers with different scope, so the lower second cost is partly reuse and partly a smaller job." Know which numbers you controlled and which finance reported. See the [Accenture cost method](../vacancies/accenture-product-owner.md) for structure.

### Q4. How do you use AI and automation in project management?

**Model answer:** "For the work around decisions, not the decisions. I use AI to draft status reports and RAID updates from meeting notes, summarise long threads, and check plans for missing dependencies — then I verify, because it can sound confident and be wrong. I keep confidential client data out of public tools. And I've managed AI delivery itself: the order-automation rollout went through a pilot with defined exceptions and a measured baseline before it scaled." [1][2]

### Q5. Which methodology do you use, and how do you choose?

**Model answer:** "The one that fits the uncertainty and the governance. Fixed regulatory scope with external dependencies suits a predictive plan with stage gates; product work with changing requirements suits Scrum or Kanban; most organisations need a hybrid — Agile delivery inside funding and release governance. PMBOK 8 frames this as principles and performance domains rather than one process, which matches how I work." [1][2]

### Q6. How do you handle stakeholders who keep changing requirements?

**Model answer:** "Make the trade-off visible rather than refusing change. Agree the baseline and change route at the start, then show every request's effect on date, cost, quality and other priorities, and let the accountable person choose. When a SaaS client needed go-live in four weeks against a six-to-eight-week estimate, an impact matrix made the choice obvious: customer-facing screens first, internal admin screens later. We went live on schedule." [1]

### Q7. How do you handle a disengaged or underperforming team member?

**Model answer:** Use [B4](../shared/evidence-bank.md#behavioural-stories-owner-supplied-13-october-2026). "When I rolled out Scrum at Natoora by announcement, a respected senior engineer disengaged and others followed. I'd skipped consulting him. I had one-to-ones to understand his concerns and agreed a structural change — no-meeting Fridays — and he became one of the strongest advocates. Now I start with a private conversation to find the cause, and I bring respected voices in before announcing change." [1]

### Q8. Tell me about a project that failed and what you learned.

**Model answer:** Use [B2](../shared/evidence-bank.md#behavioural-stories-owner-supplied-13-october-2026): features delivered to specification that users didn't adopt, the investigation, the UX recommendation, and the habit you changed — involving users early and measuring adoption after release. [1]

### Q9. How do you manage a distributed or hybrid team?

**Model answer:** "Shared core hours, decisions written down where everyone can find them, and rituals that work asynchronously." Then give your real example: the SaaS work at Natoora ran with a nine-person team across the UK and Brazil. Prepare what you actually did about the time difference — where decisions were made and how work was handed over — before using it. [1]

### Q10. How do you measure benefits after the project closes?

**Model answer:** "Assign a business owner for each benefit before go-live, record the baseline, and agree when and how it will be measured. Say what kind of benefit it is — cash released, cost avoided or time freed. The Smart Timesheet's reported £500k a year and the infrastructure programme's £400k are savings estimates; I'd explain how each was calculated rather than present them as budgets I managed."

### Q11. For public-sector roles — how should I answer behaviour questions?

**Model answer:** Prepare one STAR example for each behaviour the advert names, usually from Seeing the Big Picture, Changing and Improving, Making Effective Decisions, Leadership, Communicating and Influencing, Working Together, Developing Self and Others, Managing a Quality Service and Delivering at Pace. Lead with your own actions and the result. Good matches: Delivering at Pace — the four-week SaaS go-live; Making Effective Decisions — the B1 resource conflict; Changing and Improving — the Jira and Agile operating model; Developing Self and Others — mentoring 20+ associates. [3]

## First 90 days and panel questions

First validate the business case, commitments, governance, baseline and urgent risks. Next establish a credible forecast and resolve priority dependencies. Then improve reporting/decision cadence, transition readiness and benefits ownership. Tailor this to initiation, delivery or recovery rather than imposing a standard mobilisation template.

Ask: Which tolerances are delegated to the PM? Who approves scope and budget changes? What evidence is required at release gates? How are suppliers governed? Who owns benefits after closure?

**Rehearsal:** one regulated delivery, one change request, one recovery, one budget/forecast discussion and one operational handover. Use the [shared handbook](../shared/handbook.md) for methods; keep the project controls specific to the actual vacancy.

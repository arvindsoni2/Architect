Show how you help teams deliver useful outcomes through better flow, collaboration and learning. Seniority appears in coaching judgement, cross-team coordination and removing structural constraints. Running ceremonies alone does not demonstrate that impact.

Positioning and evidence

A useful opening: “My experience combines Agile delivery leadership with product ownership and technical delivery. I help teams make work and dependencies visible, improve collaboration and inspect whether changes actually improve delivery. I adapt the working approach to the service and constraints rather than treating a framework as the objective.” Add a concrete improvement with a baseline and result you can defend.

Questions to rehearse

  1. When would you choose Scrum or Kanban? Consider the nature of work, interruption rate, planning horizon and team needs. Scrum provides goals and bounded inspection cycles; Kanban makes flow explicit and controls work in progress. Explain the relevant practices and review evidence rather than declaring one universally superior.
  2. How do you improve predictability? Make work and policies visible, reduce excessive work in progress, split work, expose blockers and use observed completion data. Report uncertainty and dependencies. Predictability must preserve quality and user value.
  3. What metrics do you use? Explain lead/cycle time boundaries, throughput, work-item age, WIP and quality measures. Discuss trends and variability. Velocity is team-specific planning information, not a cross-team ranking or productivity target.
  4. How do you facilitate a difficult retrospective? Create safety, use concrete evidence, invite quieter voices, distinguish systemic constraints from blame and choose a small experiment with an owner and review date. Explain what changed in a real example.
  5. How do you coach a team resisting change? Understand the problem and their experience, co-design a low-risk experiment, make the benefit visible and inspect it. Explain when a policy is mandatory and when the team has choice.
  6. How do you handle repeated sprint spillover? Examine work slicing, scope changes, hidden work, dependencies, quality and capacity. Collaborate with the PO and Developers on a realistic Sprint Goal and planning approach. Do not simply demand higher estimates or overtime.
  7. How do you coordinate several teams? Map shared outcomes, dependencies, interface owners and decision dates. Resolve the constraint closest to the work; use programme forums for decisions beyond team authority. Attendance at a scaled ceremony alone is not evidence of leading scaled delivery.
  8. How do you work with a PO, engineering lead and manager? Clarify accountability for value, technical quality, people support and delivery facilitation. Surface trade-offs jointly. Avoid becoming the sole approver or a messenger for every decision.

Coaching answer exercise

Select a real case of excessive work in progress, delayed decisions or poor hand-offs. State your observation, the team's interpretation, a practice you agreed and the result. The SaaS portfolio describes distributed working and reusable onboarding; explain which parts you personally established and how you assessed them. The two-client cost and duration comparison is useful evidence, but does not prove that ceremonies caused a productivity increase.

Use: “We observed [specific problem]. I facilitated [actual discussion/experiment]. We changed [policy/working arrangement]. We checked [measure] over [period], and learned [result/next change].” Complete this with real evidence before rehearsal.

Flow scenario — illustrative, not historical

Ten items are in progress, only two finish each week, and urgent support work repeatedly interrupts feature delivery. Clarify whether support and planned work share skills and capacity. Visualise all work and item age; establish explicit urgency criteria. Test lower WIP, smaller slices, clearer intake or a reserved support approach with the team. Avoid making a new support lane a dumping ground that conceals work.

Use throughput and observed cycle-time distribution to discuss the likely completion range. Review quality, user outcomes and team experience as guardrails. If the bottleneck is another department's approval, coach locally while escalating that structural constraint with evidence. Do not promise a fixed percentage improvement before observing the system.

Seniority and credibility probes

Prepare examples of influencing outside your team, coaching another practitioner, challenging an unhelpful metric and changing your own approach after feedback. State actual mentoring scope: formal mentoring, peer coaching and team facilitation are related but different experiences.

Expect: How did the team choose the change? What resistance did you encounter? What would have happened without it? How did you distinguish correlation from causation? Did improvement endure after you stepped back? When did you escalate rather than facilitate?

If asked about SAFe, explain the environment you actually used, team and programme backlog responsibilities and dependency planning. Do not invent PI Planning experience. Use the shared handbook for current role distinctions.

Additional scenario drills

These are fictional practice cases. Use the shared scenario method; make the team's participation and any structural escalation explicit.

AD1 — rising velocity, falling reliability. Leadership celebrates higher story points, but releases are less predictable and defects are growing. Check whether estimation changed, work is being split differently or rework is omitted. Examine item age, blocked time, completed outcomes and escaped defects. Recommend one experiment with the team, such as smaller batches or resolving a recurring approval delay; agree quality guardrails and a review period. Probe: what if leadership insists on comparing team velocity? Weak response: chasing a points target or assuming the team needs more ceremonies.

AD2 — conflict around urgent work. Product wants a date commitment, engineering flags technical risk, and support interruptions keep invalidating plans. Clarify user impact, urgency rules, capacity and decision rights. Facilitate explicit trade-offs between planned work and service obligations; test intake policies or reserved capacity rather than enforcing a universal rule. Track interruption demand, ageing work and service impact. Probe: what if urgent work is genuinely unpredictable? Weak response: promising to eliminate interruptions or deciding the team's technical plan for them.

Bridge questions

These questions cross from your story into the system behind it and back. Each answer follows the five beats in Connect the dots (Stakes, Split, Sketch, Shape, Show); the decisions behind them are in the decision lists.

  1. How did you change engineering practice without owning engineering? “At Natoora I owned the operating model, not the pipeline. I proposed feature flags, with examples from my own engineering work, and the team built them and found them useful; I coached DevOps practice, and engineering owned CI/CD. The change that unlocked it was a free, reversible pilot of Jira on one initiative, which the CTO and COO approved once they saw the evidence.”
  2. How did you measure whether the new way of working helped? “I set a baseline before the Jira move and measured again three and six months after. Time-to-market, from starting work to release, fell from over five weeks to under three. I'm careful to say the tool was the small part: one source of truth, smaller batches and feature flags did the work. Next I'd add the DORA measures: deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate and time to restore.”

First 90 days and panel questions

Learn the service, team agreements, demand, dependencies and baseline flow first. Agree one focused improvement experiment next. Then assess the result, build team capability and tackle cross-team constraints with leadership. Preserve useful existing practices; change what the evidence justifies.

Ask: What slows teams most today? How much influence does this role have beyond the team? Which metrics trigger decisions? How are operational interruptions handled? What coaching support exists for other delivery practitioners?

Rehearsal: one facilitation conflict, one flow improvement, one coaching example, one cross-team dependency and one failed experiment. Each needs a personal action and evidence, not a ceremony list.
